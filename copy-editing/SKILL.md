# Copy editing

Establish editing depth, audience, channel, voice and protected wording from the
request and existing style guide. A proofread calls for surface corrections;
substantive editing may change argument/order. Preserve authorial intent and
identify changes that require a decision rather than silently replacing it.

Read the full draft before polishing sentences. Check its central promise,
structure, reader action and internal contradictions. Then edit for precise words,
useful transitions, consistent terminology and channel constraints. Retain useful
technical detail and distinctive voice; do not enforce a generic tone or word quota.

Check factual, numerical and comparative claims against supplied authorities or
current primary sources when needed. Distinguish a verified correction from an
editorial suggestion. Mark unsupported claims and missing evidence instead of
making them sound more confident. Preserve meaning in quotations; do not fabricate
customer approval, citations, testimonials, endorsements or legal compliance.

Give the edited draft in the requested format, plus a short change rationale and
claim questions only where they matter. Use [the review template](assets/review-template.md)
for a substantial edit and [the example](references/example.md) for ambiguity.
Check links, names, units, dates and calls to action within the actual review scope.
A word-count or channel check reports the convention/tool used when limits matter.

Complete when the requested edit is usable, consequential meaning changes are
visible and unresolved factual/approval questions have an owner or clear status.
Editing does not authorize publication.
