# User Research

## Objective and inputs

Answer a product/user question with an appropriate study or a faithful synthesis of
supplied research. Establish decision, population, known evidence, authorized data,
research/participant scope and outputs. A study plan does not authorize recruitment,
recording, contacting participants or uploading transcripts.

## Procedure

1. Turn assumptions into research questions and choose a proportionate method.
   Distinguish behavioral observation, interview report, stakeholder hypothesis and
   existing analytics. Use the [method](references/method.md) for sampling and synthesis.
2. Specify participant criteria, recruitment/sampling bias, consent/usage/recording
   needs, accommodations, study tasks/questions and analysis plan. Do not invent
   participants, a universal sample size or access to a customer panel.
3. Collect only within actual authorization using approved tools; otherwise deliver
   the plan or use supplied permitted artifacts. De-identify reporting copies,
   respect quote/retention/sharing restrictions and keep source provenance.
4. For each usable observation retain participant/session ID, method/context,
   actual behavior or quote reference, interpretation and confidence basis. Preserve
   contradictions, failures and negative cases; separate what users said from did.
5. Code themes transparently and connect them to source evidence, segment/context,
   exceptions and competing explanations. Do not turn a convenience sample into
   population prevalence, statistical certainty or a confirmed purchase intention.
6. Map supported insights to product decisions/hypotheses and follow-up validation.
   Keep business desirability, usability and feasibility distinct. Use existing
   product/design/analytics methods when available, without treating study findings
   as automatic requirements or permission to change a product.
7. Deliver the [study/synthesis report](assets/report-template.md), provenance and
   usage limits, evidence/theme matrix, uncertainty, decision implications and actual
   completed versus planned work. Mark unperformed sessions and unanswered questions.

## Completion

Every insight has a traceable permitted source and contradictory evidence remains
visible. Reports do not fabricate research, overgeneralize the sample, expose private
participants or claim a plan is an executed study. No outreach is sent implicitly.
